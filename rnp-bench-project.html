RNP Bench

A workbench for RNA–protein molecular dynamics that runs entirely in your browser. It prepares a complex, checks what would break the topology build, writes inputs for GROMACS, AMBER and OpenMM, and analyses the trajectory that comes back.

Open RNP Bench Try it on 1URN

1URN is the U1A protein bound to its RNA hairpin, the textbook RRM–RNA complex and small enough to explore quickly.

From structure to trajectory in seven steps

  1. Load

    PDB, mmCIF or GROMACS files, or any released entry fetched from RCSB. Multi-model files open as a trajectory.

  2. Structure

    A cartoon viewer with secondary structure, and checks for gaps, missing side chains, non-standard residues and clashes.

  3. Interface

    Contacts, salt bridges to phosphate, hydrogen bonds, stacking, Watson–Crick pairs and buried surface area.

  4. Physics

    Collective motions tested against crystallographic B-factors, screened electrostatics, and RNA sugar pucker and backbone torsions.

  5. System plan

    Force field, box, water model, ions and a cost estimate, before anything is built.

  6. Input files

    Ready-to-run inputs for GROMACS, AMBER and OpenMM, downloadable together, with a check that the force field has a template for every residue before you queue anything.

  7. Trajectory

    Bring the frames back and analyse them: 3D playback, RMSD, RMSF, radius of gyration, contact and hydrogen-bond occupancy, base-pair survival and RNA torsions over time.

Where the simulation runs

Molecular dynamics needs a real engine on real hardware, which a website does not have and should not pretend to. So the simulation is the one step RNP Bench deliberately leaves to you.

Prepare

In your browser. Structure checks, interface and physics analysis, the system plan, and input files for all three engines.

download

Run

Wherever you have the hardware: a lab GPU, a university cluster, a free GPU notebook. Nothing here touches your machine or mine.

upload

Analyse

Back in your browser. Load the trajectory and every analysis works on it, whichever engine produced it.

Nothing you load is uploaded anywhere: structures and trajectories stay in the browser. That also makes the copy on this site a working tool rather than a screenshot, since only the run step ever needed a server.

I run my own simulations through the same page, served by its OpenMM backend on a lab GPU over an SSH tunnel. The page notices when that backend is there and offers a Run step; on a public site like this one, it does not.

A run recorded on the lab GPU

Since the run step is not available here, I recorded one and published its frames and energy curves. RNP Bench loads them straight into the trajectory step, so you can try the analysis without running anything first.

Design choices

Private by default

Nothing you load leaves the browser. The optional OpenMM backend listens on localhost; put it on a network and it generates a token, stops accepting browser origins it was not told about, and refuses systems above an atom limit.

Engine-agnostic

The analysis reads a plain multi-model PDB, so it does not care which engine produced the trajectory. Runs on the OpenMM backend also download as one archive with the fully parameterised system, the integrator and its seed, and a script to continue.

Statistics that admit their limits

Torsions use circular statistics, drift is tested against the scatter within each half of the run, and interaction energies are reported as interaction energies, not binding free energies.

AI review, human decisions

Gemini sees a few kilobytes of summary numbers, never coordinates. Its proposals arrive as cards to apply or reject, and every decision goes into a log.
